10 Ways to Improve Your English Communication Skills

Discover practical ways to improve your English speaking, listening, writing and confidence in everyday communication.

Introduction

English communication skills are important in education, professional life and everyday conversations. Good communication helps you express your ideas, understand others and build confidence.

Improving your English requires regular practice, patience and consistency.

Why Are English Communication Skills Important?

Strong communication skills help you participate in interviews, presentations, meetings and conversations confidently.

Quick Tip: Spend at least 20 minutes every day practising English.

10 Ways to Improve Your English Communication Skills

1. Read Every Day

Read English newspapers, books, blogs and articles. Reading improves vocabulary, grammar and sentence formation.

2. Listen Carefully

Listen to English podcasts, interviews, news and educational videos.

3. Practice Speaking

Speak English with friends, classmates or colleagues. You can also practise speaking in front of a mirror.

4. Learn New Vocabulary

Learn five new English words every day and use them in your own sentences.

5. Improve Your Writing

Write short paragraphs, daily journal entries and emails in English.

6. Watch English Videos

Watch English movies and educational videos to improve your understanding.

7. Improve Your Grammar

Learn grammar rules, including tenses, prepositions, voice, narration, and sentence structures.

📖 Quick Resource: Complete handwritten English grammar notes are digitized and available in our dedicated notes section below. Jump to Grammar Notes ↓

8. Think in English

Try describing your daily activities in English instead of translating every sentence.

9. Ask for Feedback

Ask teachers and friends to correct your mistakes.

10. Build Your Confidence

Do not be afraid of making mistakes. Every mistake is an opportunity to learn.

Conclusion

Improving English communication skills requires regular practice and dedication. Start with small daily activities and gradually build your confidence.

Remember: Keep learning, keep speaking and keep improving!

CHAPTER 01 Foundations

Grammar & Orthography (वर्ण विचार)

Definition of grammar, 5 branches, 26 letters, Vowels, Consonants, Semi-vowels (W, Y), and Alphabet order.

1.1 Definition of Grammar (व्याकरण की परिभाषा)

English: Grammar is the process or system of learning a language correctly in its spoken and written forms.

हिन्दी: व्याकरण वह साधन है जिसके माध्यम से हम किसी भाषा को शुद्ध-शुद्ध बोलना, पढ़ना और लिखना सीखते हैं।

1.2 Five Branches of Grammar (व्याकरण के 5 अंग)

  • 1. Orthography (वर्ण विचार): Deals with letters, phonetic sounds, spellings, vowels, and consonants.
  • 2. Etymology (शब्द विचार): Deals with words, derivation, morphemes, and word formation.
  • 3. Syntax (वाक्य विचार): Deals with sentence construction, subject-verb agreement, and word order.
  • 4. Punctuation (विराम चिन्ह विचार): Deals with stops, marks, and clarity in writing.
  • 5. Rhetoric and Prosody (अलंकार एवं छंद विचार): Deals with literary embellishment, rhythm, and poetic meter.

1.3 Orthography & Classification of Letters (कुल 26 वर्ण)

Vowels (स्वर - 5):

a, e, i, o, u — Pronounced independently without the aid of any other sound (वह वर्ण जो बिना किसी अन्य ध्वनि की सहायता से बोला जाए).

Consonants (व्यंजन - 19/21):

b, c, d, f, g, h, j, k, l, m, n, p, q, r, s, t, v, x, z — Pronounced with the help of a vowel (e.g. b = b + e, f = a + f).

Semi-Vowels (अर्ध स्वर): W and Y

• At Beginning: Pronounced as Consonant ('य' / 'व') -> Yadav, Yellow, Wolf, Woman

• In Middle/End: Pronounced as Vowel ('ई', 'आई', 'ऑ', 'आउ') -> City, Cry, Law, Cow

CHAPTER 02 Morphology

Etymology & Morphemes (शब्द विचार एवं शब्द निर्माण)

Definition of words, Morphemes (Prefix, Suffix, Infix), and Form Classes vs Structure Classes.

2.1 Word & Morpheme (शब्द एवं शब्दांश)

Word (शब्द): A group of letters with a clear semantic meaning (e.g. p + e + n = pen ✓ vs npe ✗).

Morpheme (Affix): The smallest grammatical unit that alters the meaning of a root word without having independent dictionary meaning on its own.

2.2 The Three Types of Morphemes

KindHindiPositionExamples
Prefixउपसर्गBeginning (Pre + fix)im- + possible = impossible, un- + happy = unhappy
Suffixप्रत्ययEnd (Suff + fix)drive + -er = driver, quick + -ly = quickly
Infixअंतःप्रत्ययMiddle modificationman -> men, foot -> feet, tooth -> teeth

2.3 Form Classes vs Structure Classes

• Form Classes (रूप वर्ग): Words that change form by adding morphemes (Noun, Adjective, Verb, Adverb) — e.g. clever -> cleverer, cleverest, cleverly, cleverness.

• Structure Classes (संरचना वर्ग): Grammatical words with fixed forms (Pronouns, Prepositions, Conjunctions, Helping Verbs, Determiners, Intensifiers).

CHAPTER 03 Parts of Speech

Parts of Speech (शब्द भेद - रूप एवं संरचना वर्ग)

Noun categories, Pronoun cases (Subjective, Objective, Possessive), Preposition rules (English vs Hindi), Conjunctions, and Auxiliaries.

3.1 Noun Classification

  • Proper Noun (व्यक्तिवाचक): Specific names (Ram, Ranchi, The Ganga).
  • Countable Noun (गणनीय): Things counted individually (Book, Pen, Boy). Has plural form and takes a/an.
  • Uncountable Noun (अगणनीय): Things eaten, drunk, felt, or measured (Water, Milk, Rice, Bread, Honesty). Always singular; never takes 's/es' or 'a/an' directly.

3.2 Master Pronoun Case Table

1st PersonI / Weme / usmy / ourmine / ours
2nd PersonYouyouyouryours
3rd PersonHe / She / They / Ithim / her / them / ithis / her / their / itshis / hers / theirs / —

Difference: "This is my book." (Possessive Adjective) vs "This book is mine." (Possessive Pronoun).

3.3 Key Structure Words

• Preposition (संबंध सूचक): Placed before the noun in English, but after it in Hindi: "वह पटना से आ रहा है।" -> "He is coming from Patna."

• Conjunction (समुच्चय बोधक): Joining words (and, but, because, although, or, nor).

• Intensifier (तीव्रक): Words like very, hardly, rarely, extremely used to intensify adjectives/verbs.

Basic structure (S + V + O) and detailed analysis of Assertive, Interrogative, Imperative, Optative, and Exclamatory sentences.

CHAPTER 05 Core Engine

Master Guide to Tenses (काल - सम्पूर्ण 12 रूप एवं विशेष संरचनाएँ)

All 12 tenses with Hindi identification, routine, continuity, exclusivity (did nothing but), inception (began to), Past Perfect before/after, and inversions.

5.1 The 8 Special Structures of Simple Present

  • Type I (State): S + is/am/are + Comp -> "वह गरीब है।" -> He is poor.
  • Type II (Intention): S + is/am/are + to + V1 -> "वह जाने को है।" -> He is to go.
  • Type III (Possession): S + has/have + Comp -> "राम के पास एक खिलौना है।" -> Ram has a toy.
  • Type IV (Compulsion): S + has/have + to + V1 -> "मुझे जाना पड़ता है।" -> I have to go.
  • Type V (Habit / Routine): S + V1/V5 + O -> "वह खेलता है।" -> He plays football.
  • Type VI (Continuity): S + keeps on / goes on + V4 -> "वह पढ़ता रहता है।" -> He keeps on reading.
  • Type VII (Exclusivity): S + does/do nothing but + V1 -> "वह हँसता ही रहता है।" -> He does nothing but laugh.
  • Type VIII (Inception): S + begins to + V1 -> "वह रोने लगती है।" -> She begins to weep.

5.2 Crucial Past & Future Tense Rules

Past Perfect with BEFORE and AFTER:

• Before: 1st Action (Past Perfect) + before + 2nd Action (Simple Past)
-> "डॉक्टर के आने के पहले रोगी मर चुका था।" -> The patient had died before the doctor came.

• After: 2nd Action (Simple Past) + after + 1st Action (Past Perfect)
-> "डॉक्टर के आने के बाद रोगी मरा।" -> The patient died after the doctor had come.

• Inversion: No sooner did I reach the station than the train started.

• Since vs For: Since for Point of Time (since morning, since 1999); For for Period of Time (for two days, for three hours).

• Stative Verbs: Verbs like know, understand, believe, love are NOT used in continuous tense ("I know you", not "I am knowing you").

CHAPTER 06 Auxiliaries

Modal Verbs (विधिवाचक सहायक क्रियाएँ)

Can, Could, May, Might, Should, Must, Ought to, Would, Used to, Dare, Need, Purpose clauses (so that/lest), and Modal Perfects.

Core Modal Formula: Subject + Modal Auxiliary + V1 (Base Form) + Object

Purpose Clauses (उद्देश्य सूचक वाक्य):

• So that (ताकि): "वह कड़ी मेहनत करता है ताकि उसे सफलता मिले।" -> He labours hard so that he may get success.

• Lest (ताकि...न): Lest + S + should + V1 -> He labours hard lest he should fail. (Never use 'not' with lest).

• Modal Perfect: S + modal + have + V3 -> "मैं कर सकता था।" -> I could have done it. | "उसे जाना चाहिए था।" -> He should have gone.

CHAPTER 07 Emphasis

Emphatic Sentences (जोरदार वचन)

Converting simple statements into emphatic sentences using Do, Does, Did, emphatic have/has/had, and imperative emphasis.

Concept: Adding special stress, certainty, or force to an action.

• "मैं पढ़ता हूँ।" -> I read. (Non-emphatic / साधारण कथन)
• "मैं तो / अवश्य पढ़ता हूँ।" -> I do read. (Emphatic / जोरदार कथन)

CHAPTER 08 Conversation

Question Tags (पुछल्ले प्रश्न)

Core rules, negative statements, special tags (aren't I?, shall we?), negative words (hardly, few, little), and indefinite pronouns.

The Golden Rule of Question Tags: Positive Statement -> Negative Tag | Negative Statement -> Positive Tag

Core Examples & Special Rules

  • "Ram is playing, isn't he?" | "I don't like him, do I?"
  • Special 'I am': "I am right, aren't I?" (Not: amn't I ✗).
  • Special 'Let us': "Let us go for a walk, shall we?"
  • Negative Words: Words like hardly, scarcely, seldom, rarely, few, little make the sentence negative, requiring a positive tag:
    -> "He hardly goes to school, does he?"
    -> "I have few books, have I?" (vs "I have a few books, haven't I?")
  • Indefinite Pronouns: Somebody, nobody, everyone, anyone take the pronoun they in the tag:
    -> "Somebody has done it, haven't they?" | "Nobody is perfect, are they?"
CHAPTER 09 Syntax Tools

Introductory 'It' & 'There' (डमी / परिचयात्मक कर्ता)

Usage of 'It' for time, weather, seasons, temporary subjects, and 'There' for existence, presence, and exclusivity ('nothing but').

9.1 Usages of Introductory 'IT'

  • Time & Day: "आज शनिवार है।" -> It is Saturday today. | "अभी 8 बजे हैं।" -> It is 8 o'clock.
  • Weather & Nature: "वर्षा हो रही है।" -> It is raining. | "जाड़े का मौसम है।" -> It is winter.
  • Temporary Subject: "टहलना लाभदायक है।" -> It is useful to walk. (Equivalent to: To walk is useful).
  • Emphatic Identification: "मैं ही तो हूँ।" -> It is I. (Not: It is me).

9.2 Usages of Introductory 'THERE'

• Existence / Location: "एक बालक है।" -> There is a boy. | "पेड़ पर बंदर थे।" -> There were monkeys on the tree.

• Exclusivity ('ही' / Nothing but): When nothing else exists except that object (the verb remains singular is/was):
-> "पेड़ पर बंदर ही बंदर हैं।" -> There is nothing but monkeys on the tree.
-> "रेगिस्तान में बालू ही बालू है।" -> There is nothing but sand in the desert.

CHAPTER 10 Adjectives

Degrees of Comparison (विशेषण की तुलनात्मक अवस्थाएँ)

Positive, Comparative, Superlative, formation rules, Latin '-ior to' adjectives, absolute adjectives, and parallel increase (The higher... the colder...).

10.1 The 3 Degrees of Adjectives

• Positive: "Ram is a clever boy." | "He is as clever as Ram."

• Comparative: "Ram is cleverer than Shyam." | "Gita is more beautiful than Sima."

• Superlative: "Ram is the cleverest boy." | "She is the most beautiful girl."

10.2 Special Exam Rules

Latin Adjectives ending in -ior:

Junior, Senior, Superior, Inferior, Prior, Preferable strictly take to, never 'than' or 'more':
-> Correct: "He is junior to me." ✓ (Incorrect: "junior than me" ✗ / "more junior" ✗)
-> Correct: "Tea is preferable to coffee." ✓

Parallel Increase / Decrease: The + Comparative Degree ..., the + Comparative Degree ...

• "तुम जितना अधिक मेहनत करोगे उतना अधिक सफल बनोगे।" -> The more you labour, the more successful you become.

• "The higher we go, the colder we feel."

CHAPTER 11 Voice

Active & Passive Voice (वाच्य - नियम, तालिका एवं अपवाद)

Master 8-tense transformation chart, Modal passives, Infinitives (to be + V3), Imperatives (Let...be), Non-'by' prepositions (known to), and story exercise.

General Passive Structure: Object (as Subject) + Helping Verb + V3 (Past Participle) + by + Subject (as Object)

Preposition Exceptions (Verbs Not Taking 'BY')

  • Know -> to: "I know you." -> You are known to me. (Not: known by me ✗).
  • Satisfy / Please -> with: "Your work satisfies me." -> I am satisfied with your work.
  • Surprise / Shock -> at: "The news shocked him." -> He was shocked at the news.
  • Interest -> in: "Social work interests me." -> I am interested in social work.
CHAPTER 12 Syntax

Subject-Verb Agreement (कर्ता-क्रिया अन्विति - 23 सम्पूर्ण नियम)

The fundamental concord law (S-S, P-P), compound subjects, pair nouns, either/or, neither/nor, collective nouns, relative pronouns, and quantifiers.

The Fundamental Law of Concord: Singular Subject -> Singular Verb (S -> S) | Plural Subject -> Plural Verb (P -> P)

Key Syntax Rules from Notebook

  • Pair Words as Single Unit: "Bread and butter is my breakfast." | "Rice and Dal is my favourite dish." | "Slow and steady wins the race."
  • Or / Nor / Either...or / Neither...nor: Verb agrees with the nearest / last subject: "Ram or I am to do it." | "Neither the teacher nor the students were present."
  • The + Adjective: Plural subject: "The poor are honest." | "The rich hate the poor."
  • A Number of vs The Number of: A number of boys are playing (Plural) vs The number of boys is fifty (Singular).
  • Noun + of + Noun: Agrees with the 1st noun: "The boys of the school are honest." | "The water of the river is dirty."
  • As well as / Along with / With: Agrees with the 1st subject: "Ram, as well as I, is to do it."
  • Nouns Plural in Form but Singular in Meaning: "Mathematics is my favourite subject." | "Politics is a dirty game." | "No news is good news."
  • Always Plural Nouns: "The police have arrested the thief." | "Cattle are grazing."
CHAPTER 13 Determiners

Determiners & Articles (उपपद एवं निर्धारक)

Phonetic rules for A vs An (Hindi vowel sounds), 10 extensive usages of The, 4 omission rules (No article), Many/Much, Some/Any, and Few/Little.

Phonetic Rule for 'A' vs 'AN':

• Hindi Vowel Sound (अ, आ, इ, ई, ए, ऐ, ओ, औ) -> AN:
-> An umbrella (sound 'अ'), An honest boy (sound 'ऑ'), An M.A. (sound 'एम'), An S.P. (sound 'एस'), An F.I.R.

• Hindi Consonant Sound (क, ख, ग, य, व...) -> A:
-> A university (sound 'य'), A European (sound 'य'), A one-rupee note (sound 'व'), A B.A. (sound 'बी').

10 Key Usages of 'THE' (Definite Article)

Before Superlatives (the tallest), Plural Adjectives (the poor), Noun of Noun (the water of river), Parallel Comparison (the higher... the colder), Whole Class (the cow), Ordinals (the first), Unique Bodies (the Sun, the Earth), Geographical Rivers/Oceans/Ranges (the Ganga, the Himalayas), Monuments & Holy Books (the Taj Mahal, the Ramayana), and Union Countries (the Punjab, the USA, the UK).

Omission of Articles (No Article)

• No article before general uncountables (water, gold), general plurals (boys), meals (breakfast), games (cricket), subjects/languages (English, Math), days/months (Sunday, January), and after kind of / type of.

CHAPTER 14 Speech

Direct & Indirect Speech (प्रत्यक्ष एवं अप्रत्यक्ष कथन - Narration)

Reporting Verb & Reported Speech, Tense shift rules, The S-O-N Formula for person transformation, Time words, Assertive, Interrogative (do/does/did rules), and Imperatives.

The S-O-N Formula for Person Transformation: 1 -> S (1st Person -> Subject) | 2 -> O (2nd Person -> Object) | 3 -> N (3rd Person -> No Change)

• He said to me, "I will help you." -> He told me that he would help me.

• Gita said to me, "You will return my book." -> Gita told me that I would return her book.

Tense Shift Law & Exceptions

• Past Reporting Verb: V1/V5 -> V2, is/am/are -> was/were, has/have -> had, V2 -> had + V3, shall/will -> should/would, can -> could, may -> might.

• Universal Truths & Habits (NO CHANGE): He said, "God is everywhere." -> He said that God is everywhere. | "Honesty is the best policy." -> Honesty is the best policy.

Sentence-Specific Special Rules

  • Assertive: said to -> told; Linker: that.
  • Interrogative (Yes/No): said to -> asked; Linker: if / whether. (Remove do/does -> V2; did -> had + V3):
    -> The doctor said, "Did you take medicine?" -> The doctor asked if he had taken the medicine.
  • Interrogative (Wh-): The Question Word is itself the linker (Why, What, Where, How).
  • Imperative: ordered, requested, advised, forbade + to + V1:
    -> She said, "Don't disturb me." -> She forbade me to disturb her. (or prohibited me from disturbing her).
  • Optative: wished / prayed that + S + might + V1: "May God help you!" -> He prayed that God might help me.
CHAPTER 15 Transformation

Transformation of Sentences (वाक्य रूपांतरण - अर्थ बदले बिना रूप परिवर्तन)

Interchange of degrees of comparison, Removal of 'Too...to', Assertive <-> Interrogative, Affirmative <-> Negative (antonyms, None but), and Exclamatory <-> Assertive.

15.1 Removal of 'Too...to' (Remove Too)

S + V + SO + Adjective + THAT + S + CANNOT / COULD NOT + V1 + O

• "Ram is too weak to walk." -> Ram is so weak that he cannot walk.

• "Girls were too proud to help me." -> Girls were so proud that they could not help me.

• "He is too poor." -> He is poorer than he should be. (more than desirable).

15.2 Degree Transformation Patterns

• Superlative: Ram is the cleverest boy in our school.
• Comparative: Ram is cleverer than any other boy in our school.
• Positive: No other boy in our school is as clever as Ram.

15.3 Affirmative to Negative (Without Changing Meaning)

  • By Antonyms: "My friend is wise." -> My friend is not foolish.
  • Only / Alone -> None but: "Only Ram can do it." -> None but Ram can do it.
  • As soon as -> No sooner...than: "As soon as I went out, it began to rain." -> No sooner did I go out than it began to rain.

15.4 Exclamatory to Assertive

• "Alas! He is no more." -> It is a matter of sorrow that he is no more.

• "What a beautiful place!" -> It is a very beautiful place.

• "O that I were a bird!" -> I strongly wish that I were a bird.
